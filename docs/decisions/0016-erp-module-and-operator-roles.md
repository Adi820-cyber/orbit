# ADR 0016: Hospital operations (ERP) module and the `admin` / `hospital` operator roles

- **Status:** Proposed — implemented (merged to `main` of `ghanshamrna27-source/Pragyan`) at the product owner's request, and verified locally (see "Verification"). It changes release-one scope (PRD §5.2: "a system-of-record replacement"), the membership model, and adds a schema, so it needs sign-off before merge: **Aditya** (scope, authorization, the two accounts), **Maruti** (migration, RLS, seed), **Ayas** (ERP screens).
- **Author:** Ghansham
- **Date opened:** 2026-10-01
- **Specification:** [docs/orbit/ERP_PLAN.md](../orbit/ERP_PLAN.md). This ADR records what was actually decided and built, including where it departs from the plan.

## Context

The product owner asked for an ERP inside Orbit holding patient details, doctor details, worker details with in/out times, and the services each hospital provides, with two new sign-ins: a **hospital** account and an **admin** account.

Orbit v1 deliberately held no patient or employee identity (PRD §1.1, §5.2). The plan's decision table (ERP_PLAN §2.1, D1–D8) listed what had to be settled before building. This ADR settles each one the way it was built, so a reviewer can accept or reverse them individually.

## Decisions

### §1 Scope (D1, D7): a synthetic demonstration module, in this monorepo

- Records are **synthetic only**, generated by `packages/data-gen` from invented name lists with visibly fictional codes (`DEMO-MRN-…`, `DEMO-REG-…`). No real data, no imports, no integrations.
- Out of scope: payroll, leave balances, billing and invoicing, pharmacy, inventory, clinical content (diagnoses, notes, orders, results), and patient or staff self-service.
- Everything lives in a new **`orbit_erp`** schema. Leader tables, contracts and screens are unchanged apart from the routing described in §2.

### §2 Two operator roles, kept apart from the 14 workbook roles (D2)

| Role | Scope | May |
|---|---|---|
| `admin` | group (the whole organization) | Everything below, for every facility, plus: create and edit staff and doctors, credentials and consultation slots; maintain the service catalogue and first-time availability; change past rosters; back-date a punch (31 days max); decide attendance corrections (never their own); read the ERP audit trail |
| `hospital` | one facility | Register and correct patients; open, close and cancel visits; record and cancel services delivered; punch staff in and out; set today's and future rosters; switch a configured service on or off; request attendance corrections |

- A membership row now has **either** `role_id` (a workbook role) **or** `operator_role`, never both (CHECK `org_memberships_exactly_one_kind`). `ROLE_IDS` still lists exactly the 14 workbook roles.
- A trigger requires `admin` to hold group scope and `hospital` facility scope.
- The API keeps the two kinds apart by type: the auth hook sets `request.membership` for a leader and `request.operator` for an operator. Every leader route reads `membershipOf()` and refuses an operator with `403 forbidden`. Every ERP route reads `operatorOf()` and refuses a leader the same way. No leader code path accepts an operator, so no fallback role exists anywhere.
- Operator claims carry `operatorRole` and no `role`, so `orbit.current_role_id()` is null and every leader RLS policy matches nothing for them.
- `orbit.permitted_assignees()` now excludes operator memberships, so a leader action can never be assigned to an ERP account.
- `/api/me` answers either shape (`IdentityResponseSchema`). The web app sends an operator to `/erp` and a leader away from it.
- **Departure from the plan:** the plan proposed four operator roles (`front-desk`, `hr-admin`, `facility-admin`, `service-admin`). The product owner asked for two; the table above maps their duties onto those two.

### §3 Leaders get no ERP access (ERP_PLAN §4.2, D8)

- Leaders see no patient, staff or attendance row, and Ask Orbit never receives ERP data.
- Whether People Executive and HR Head may read individual staff rows (PRD §4 says aggregates only) is **left closed**: they cannot.
- Feeding ERP facts into workbook KPIs (D6) is **not built**; it needs Maruti's derivations and reconciliation with the KPI dataset.

### §4 Data minimization (D3)

| Record | Fields |
|---|---|
| Patients | MRN, fictional name, sex, birth **year**, status, home facility |
| Staff | Code, fictional name, type, designation, department, facility, employment status and dates, critical-role flag |
| Doctors | Specialty, fictional registration number, credential expiry, suspended flag, employment type |

No contact, address, government-id, insurance, salary, bank or performance fields exist. Free text is limited to short correction reasons and decision notes.

### §5 Attendance (D4)

- **Punches are append-only** (INSERT and SELECT grants only). A mistake is fixed by a **correction**: requested by anyone at the facility, decided by an admin who is not the requester (CHECK `corrections_not_self_decided`). Only one correction can be pending per person and day.
- **Daily attendance is derived on read** by `orbit_erp.attendance_days()`, from roster, punches, approved corrections and `erp_settings`. It never drifts from its inputs and needs no write grant.
  - **Departure from the plan:** the plan proposed a stored `attendance_days` table.
- **Rules:**
  - A punch belongs to the nearest rostered shift whose window contains it, and an overnight shift counts toward its start date.
  - A punch outside every window stays on record but does not count. The desk is told so when it punches.
  - Missing is never zero: worked minutes are null unless there is an in and a later out.
  - Statuses: scheduled, on-duty, present, late, early-exit, missing-punch, absent, on-leave, off, unrostered.
- Every threshold lives in `orbit_erp.erp_settings`, per organization and versioned. The seeded values are illustrative and need Maruti's review: late and early grace 10 minutes, windows 120 minutes before and 240 after, credential warning 30 days.
- Times are stored as `timestamptz` and shown in the organization's time zone (UTC for Kestrion, per the company manifest). The plan's per-facility time zone was not needed.
- One planned shift per person per day. Split shifts are out of scope.

### §6 Services (D5)

- The catalogue is organization-wide; `facility_services` records where each service is offered.
- A service delivered is checked by trigger against all of these:
  - the service is offered at that facility and active;
  - the performer is active staff at the facility;
  - a doctor performer's credential is valid that day;
  - the time falls inside the visit;
  - the visit is not cancelled.
- A closed visit still accepts a late-entered service inside its window.
- **No tariffs are seeded.** The `illustrative_tariff` column exists; amounts render only where an admin sets one.

### §7 Audit

- ERP events go to a separate, append-only **`orbit_erp.audit_events`**: actor membership, operator role, action, target type and id, request id. Never the record's contents.
- Only admins read it. Every patient and visit view is recorded.
- **Departure from the plan:** the plan proposed extending `orbit.audit_events`. Its `actor_role` is a foreign key to the workbook roles and its read policy serves the leader action trail, so a separate table leaves that contract untouched.

### §8 The two sign-ins

- **Accounts:** `hospital@kestrion.demo` (Avenhurst) and `admin@kestrion.demo` (group).
- **Short IDs:** on the sign-in page the IDs `hospital` and `admin` are completed to those addresses. That only builds the email; Supabase Auth checks the password.
- **Passwords:** the product owner chose short, memorable passwords and shares them out of band. They are **not in the repository**, following ADR 0013 §2 and RULES.md. The demo owner puts them in the gitignored `supabase/.env.provisioning`, and `npm run provision:erp --workspace=@orbit/data-gen` sets them in Supabase Auth and writes the membership SQL to the gitignored `supabase/seed/local/`.
- **Risk to accept:** these passwords are short and guessable, and the web app is public. Anyone who guesses them sees synthetic records only, but can create and change ERP records in the demo. Rotate them before any external demo, or keep the accounts disabled outside demos.

### §9 Separate hosting and the shared database

- **Same database.** Hospital operations uses the same Supabase project as the leadership workspace, in its own `orbit_erp` schema (forced RLS on every table), through the same `orbit_app` role. It is **not** a separate database.
- **Separate deployments are possible.** `ORBIT_SURFACE` (API) and `VITE_APP_SURFACE` (web) select `leader`, `erp` or `all` (the default, unchanged). A `leader` API does not register `/api/erp/*` and refuses operator accounts. An `erp` API does not register the leader routes and refuses leadership accounts. Four Vercel projects, one pair each, give separate URLs, environments, CORS lists and logs. See [DEPLOYMENT.md](../orbit/DEPLOYMENT.md).
- **What it does not do.** Both API pairs still hold the same database credential, so separate hosting is not separate data. A deployment pair cannot serve the other kind of account, but a leaked `orbit_app` credential reaches both schemas. The web bundle also still contains the unused half's code; only the API and the database are the barrier.
- **Stronger options, not built (a decision for Aditya):** (a) a dedicated database role for `orbit_erp` (every policy is currently `to orbit_app`, so this means revising them), or (b) a separate Supabase project for hospital operations. Option (b) needs no code change: apply all migrations plus the organization seed `0001` to the new project and run `provision:erp` against it. It costs a project slot (ADR 0008 §4.2).
- **Applied to the dev project (2026-10-01):** migration `20261001000100` and seeds `0008`–`0010` were applied to `sxpnsnfzkpkzhsxjugde` with the Supabase CLI, **excluding** the chatbot's migration `20261001000200`. Afterwards the hosted database held 218 staff, 9,517 rosters, 8,651 punches, 1,020 patients, 2,010 visits and 3,516 services delivered, all 16 `orbit_erp` tables had RLS enabled and forced, and the 17 existing accounts and 14,688 KPI observations were unchanged. The three KPI seeds `0005`–`0007` were not re-run; the CLI only updated their recorded hashes.

## Verification (2026-10-01, local only; nothing applied to a shared project)

**Migrations and seed**
- Migration `20261001000100` applies after all 13 existing migrations on PostgreSQL 18.
- The repo's 74 database integration tests pass against it as `orbit_app` (`src/db/sources.test.ts`, `rls.test.ts`).
- Seeds 0008–0010 apply, and re-apply as no-ops. Every row passes the integrity triggers: 218 staff, 9,517 rosters, 8,651 punches, 1,020 patients, 2,010 visits, 3,516 services delivered.

**Automated tests**
- Contracts: 43 tests (10 new).
- API: 30 route tests, plus 13 ERP store tests. Ten of the store tests are integration tests that run the real SQL, RLS and triggers as `orbit_app` against Postgres. One of them found a real defect (`on_duty` was null for a not-yet-started shift), fixed in the migration.
- Data-gen: 13 tests for determinism, fiction and every trigger rule.
- Web: 20 new unit tests.

**Real browser** (Edge, 390 and 1440 px, against the real API and the seeded database)
- 47 of 47 checks passed, with zero axe WCAG 2.1 A/AA violations on every ERP page checked.
- Covered:
  - operator and leader routing;
  - explicit `out_of_scope` for another facility;
  - punch in, and the out-of-window message;
  - correction request and an admin approving it, with the day re-derived;
  - patient registration, duplicate warning, visit, service and close;
  - admin-only controls hidden from the hospital account;
  - the audit trail listing views and writes.
- Supabase Auth was replaced by a local JWKS stand-in. The API's JWT verification path was unchanged.

**Not verified:** Supabase Auth itself, the hosted Supabase project, Vercel, and the Supavisor pooler, for this module.
